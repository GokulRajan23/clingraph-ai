ClinGraph AI: Evidence from Germany

Presentation brief • Verified 12 November 2026 • Disease-independent

Problem: Physicians need to find relevant information across separate medical reports and understand how findings change over time.

EvidenceWhat it supportsProof
16.7% searching and reading
9.0% searching + 7.7% reading, as shares of observed time. 13 physicians observed for 2,205 minutes at one German university medical center.		Direct measurement of information-retrieval work; a small, single-center study.de Hoop & Neumuth, 2021

A physician describes the problem

“Wenn man alles Seite für Seite durchsuchen muss, kann man ja gleich wieder in den Papierordner gucken.”

Sven Supper, specialist in paediatrics, quoted by KBV in its 2026 results. Meaning: having to search page by page feels like returning to a paper folder. Original quotation.

One sentence for the presentation

German evidence shows that physicians still face difficulty finding relevant clinical information; ClinGraph AI aims to connect findings across reports into a chronological timeline with source citations.

Recent news: Hausärztliche Praxis, 10 November 2026: “Digitalisierung: Kliniken und Pflege hinken hinterher”.

Use accurately: The physician quotation describes an individual experience, not a national estimate. The 16.7% is calculated from the 2021 study and is not all wasted time. No source here establishes minutes spent comparing longitudinal reports or time saved by ClinGraph. Measure review time, event accuracy, and citation correctness in the MVP.
